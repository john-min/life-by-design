## Design Context

### Users

Life by Design serves a broad audience while being designed first for single professionals in their late 30s living in the Bay Area. They are thoughtful, accomplished, and interested in building a full life across relationships, community, place, play, purpose, and work—not merely optimizing a career. They use the experience privately or alongside friends to learn the high-level *Designing Your Life* framework, notice patterns, and imagine several meaningful futures.

### Brand Personality

Calm, thoughtful, and quietly optimistic. The experience should feel like a reflective journal, an engaging workshop, and a respectful editorial companion to a beloved book. It should encourage curiosity without sounding instructional, clinical, or like a productivity system.

### Aesthetic Direction

An expansive, nature-forward editorial world inspired by—but not copied from—the turquoise *Designing Your Life* cover. Warm paper grounds a palette of lake blue, deep pine, sun yellow, trail-marker red, and small constellations of colorful dots. Landscapes, horizon lines, field-note details, generous typography, and hand-made visual gestures create a sense of possibility. Light mode first. Avoid generic SaaS cards, self-help clichés, corporate wellness imagery, and neon AI gradients.

### Design Principles

1. Create spaciousness: each screen should feel like room to think, not another task to complete.
2. Teach through participation: pair every concept with one small, low-pressure action.
3. Hold multiple possibilities: avoid language that implies there is one correct life or answer.
4. Let color mean possibility: use bright dots and marks sparingly to represent paths, people, and experiments.
5. Respect the source: clearly credit the book and authors while using original language, visuals, and interaction design.
6. Design for real life: accessible touch targets, strong contrast, reduced-motion support, and excellent mobile composition are non-negotiable.
