# Life by Design — Content Overview

Life by Design is a calm, interactive introduction to the ideas behind *Designing Your Life*. It helps people notice what gives them energy, imagine multiple meaningful futures, and identify a small next experiment.

## 1. Opening

**Core message:** A full life is not simply found. It can be designed through curiosity, reflection, and experimentation.

The opening introduces the experience through an expansive lake-and-mountain landscape and invites visitors to either begin the guided reflection or explore the framework.

## 2. The Five Designer Mindsets

The experience presents the five canonical mindsets named in the book:

1. **Curiosity** — Turn judgment into questions and remain open to new possibilities.
2. **Bias to Action** — Build a way forward through small experiments that create real information.
3. **Reframing** — Examine the problem itself and change the frame when it does not support action.
4. **Awareness** — Notice where you are in the process, including when you have become stuck.
5. **Radical Collaboration** — Invite other people into the process for perspective, opportunity, and help.

## 3. The Life Design Dashboard

Visitors take a compassionate snapshot of four dimensions of a full life. Each is shown as a four-part gas tank with five possible states: 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100% full.

- **Health:** Body, mind, and emotional wellbeing.
- **Work:** Contribution, craft, and livelihood.
- **Play:** Joy, wonder, and activity without a productive purpose.
- **Love:** People, belonging, and mutual care.

There is no combined score or ideal shape. A low tank is an invitation to notice where life may need care—not evidence of failure.

## 4. Lifeview, Workview, and Coherence

Visitors write short reflections on two foundational perspectives:

- **Lifeview:** What makes life meaningful? What creates purpose, joy, and connection?
- **Workview:** What is work for? How should contribution, growth, money, service, and craft fit into a full life?

A third prompt asks where these two views reinforce one another and where they are in tension. The views are treated as evolving drafts rather than permanent personal manifestos.

## 5. The Energy Compass

Visitors record an ordinary activity and consider two dimensions:

- **Energy:** Did the activity leave them drained or energized?
- **Engagement:** Did they feel distant, present, absorbed, or in flow?

The purpose is to identify patterns through lived experience rather than judging activities by how productive or impressive they appear.

## 6. The Odyssey Plan

Visitors sketch three distinct five-year possibilities:

1. **Path One — The path they are already on:** What could happen if the current direction develops beautifully?
2. **Path Two — The path if Plan One disappeared:** What different direction would deserve serious exploration?
3. **Path Three — The path without status or constraint:** What might become interesting if money and other people's opinions became quieter?

Each path receives a short headline and a note about what makes the possibility compelling. The exercise is exploratory; visitors are not asked to choose a path.

## 7. Guided Reflection

The experience asks eight questions:

1. Where in your life do you feel most alive right now?
2. What regularly leaves you feeling depleted—even when you do it well?
3. When did you last become so absorbed that you lost track of time?
4. Beyond work, which people and roles make your life feel full?
5. What belief about your future might be ready for a gentler reframe?
6. If your current path continued beautifully, what could the next five years hold?
7. If that path disappeared tomorrow, what different life would you explore?
8. If money and other people's opinions went quiet, what would you become curious about?

The questions are intentionally reflective rather than diagnostic. There are no scores, prescribed answers, or claims that one future is correct.

## 8. Closing Invitation

The experience ends by encouraging one small conversation or experiment rather than a life-changing decision:

> What is one conversation your future self would be glad you started?

## Privacy and Attribution
