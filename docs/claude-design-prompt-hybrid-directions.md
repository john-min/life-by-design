# Claude Design Prompt — Three Hybrid Directions

Copy everything below into the existing Claude Design conversation and attach the current comparison-screen screenshot.

---

Continue from the existing **Life by Design** concept comparison shown in the attached screenshot. Keep Directions A–C as useful explorations, but now create **three additional directions—D, E, and F—that intentionally synthesize the strongest parts of all three original concepts**.

The current screen is calm, legible, and editorial. Preserve that restraint and clarity. However, it is currently too beige, too static, and too visually divided into three unrelated concepts. I want the next explorations to feel like members of one distinctive visual family.

## New shared creative foundation

Every new direction should combine:

- The quiet editorial confidence and tactile warmth of **The Field Guide**
- The routes, layers, contour lines, and spatial thinking of **The Life Atlas**
- The poetic dots, connections, and emerging patterns of **The Possibility Constellation**

The dominant new visual signature should be a **calm blue constellation language inspired by the feeling of the original book cover**—without copying its ring, exact composition, typography, or protected artwork.

Use blue much more confidently than in the current comparison screen:

- Lake blue and pale turquoise can become large atmospheric fields.
- Cobalt and clear-blue dots should make up most constellation points and route markers.
- Deep navy or blue-green ink can replace generic gray.
- Yellow, red, and green should remain as rare, meaningful punctuation—not equal-weight confetti.
- Aim for roughly 70% blue-family markers and 30% combined yellow, red, and green markers.

The constellation should not be decorative wallpaper. Every dot must be capable of representing a clue, activity, relationship, belief, conversation, experiment, or possible future. Lines appear only when the visitor discovers a meaningful connection.

## Product and content constraints

Maintain the canonical book framework already established in the prototype:

- The five designer mindsets: Curiosity, Bias to Action, Reframing, Awareness, and Radical Collaboration
- The Life Design Dashboard with four quarter-tank gauges for Health, Work, Play, and Love
- Dashboard choices limited to 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%
- Lifeview, Workview, and Coherence
- Energy and Engagement tracking
- Three Odyssey Plan possibilities
- Eight reflective questions
- A concrete Radical Collaboration action: identify someone who could help test a possible future through a 20-minute conversation

No login, public scores, social feed, AI life advice, combined life score, or gamified achievement system. Notes remain private and local to the visitor’s device.

## Direction D — The Blue Field Journal

**Creative thesis:** A quiet field notebook in which blue observations accumulate into a personal constellation.

Weight the source concepts approximately:

- 45% Field Guide
- 35% Constellation
- 20% Atlas

Use warm paper, book-like typography, fine blue rules, generous margins, handwritten-style annotations used sparingly, and large areas of pale lake blue. Replace the current dotted trail with a mostly blue constellation trail that moves between sections like marginalia.

The landscape should be present but restrained: a low horizon, distant mountain silhouettes, a lake-like wash, and a broad open sky containing blue observation points.

Signature interaction:

Each reflection creates a small blue “field mark” in the page margin. Related marks gradually connect across sections, turning the visitor’s journal into a constellation of lived clues.

Dashboard treatment:

Health, Work, Play, and Love appear as four horizontally segmented field gauges, like simple tanks drawn in a naturalist’s notebook. Filled quarters contain small clusters of blue dots rather than flat progress-bar color.

Odyssey treatment:

Three fold-out field sketches show possible routes across the same blue horizon.

This should be the calmest, most literary, and easiest-to-implement direction.

## Direction E — The Celestial Life Atlas

**Creative thesis:** A personal atlas where terrain and constellations describe the same life from two perspectives.

Weight the source concepts approximately:

- 40% Atlas
- 40% Constellation
- 20% Field Guide

Use pale turquoise map fields, cobalt route lines, restrained contour rings, coordinates, layered paper textures, and clusters of blue nodes. Avoid making it look like GPS software, a transit map, or a data dashboard.

The key visual metaphor is that a life can be read both as terrain beneath you and as a constellation above you. Terrain represents present conditions; constellations represent patterns and possibilities.

Signature interaction:

The visitor can shift between “Ground” and “Sky” views. Ground reveals the current dashboard and energy terrain. Sky reveals connections among Lifeview, Workview, relationships, and possible futures. The same blue nodes remain in place, but their connections change meaning.

Dashboard treatment:

The four quarter-tanks sit at the edges of a simple life map. Changing a tank subtly changes the density of its surrounding terrain rather than producing a score or warning.

Lifeview and Workview treatment:

Display them as two translucent blue map layers. Their overlap forms a darker area labeled “coherence,” while areas that do not overlap reveal questions worth exploring.

Odyssey treatment:

Three cobalt routes begin from the same “You are here” point and move across different combinations of terrain and stars.

This should be the most spatial, conceptually rich, and systems-oriented direction.

## Direction F — The Stillwater Constellation

**Creative thesis:** A calm lake reflects a changing sky of possibilities; what is above and what is lived gradually come into alignment.

Weight the source concepts approximately:

- 45% Constellation
- 35% Field Guide
- 20% Atlas

Use an expansive pale-blue and turquoise composition with warm-paper pauses. Introduce deeper cobalt only for meaningful connections. The interface should feel luminous and contemplative, not dark, cosmic, mystical, or futuristic.

The page alternates between open “sky” sections and grounded “shore” sections. The sky holds ideas and possibilities. The water reflects the choices and experiments the visitor has actually tried.

Signature interaction:

Potential futures first appear as loose blue stars. When a visitor identifies a real conversation or experiment, that star gains a reflection in the lake below. Only tested possibilities receive connecting lines.

Dashboard treatment:

The four quarter-tanks resemble calm water-level gauges. Each filled quarter adds a subtle horizontal band of blue and a small star reflection. Preserve explicit labels of 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%.

Radical Collaboration treatment:

A selected constellation point can become a “shared star” when the visitor names someone to speak with. Show this as two nearby blue points connected by one quiet line—not as a social network feature.

Odyssey treatment:

Three constellations appear above the same lake. As concrete prototypes are added, their reflections grow clearer below.

This should be the most emotional, atmospheric, and visually distinctive direction.
